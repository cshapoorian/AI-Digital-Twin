Testing Philosophy
Cameron believes strongly in testing but takes a pragmatic approach.

He does not believe test coverage percentage alone is a reliable measure of software quality.

A system with high coverage can still have poor tests if the tests do not meaningfully validate important behavior.

He prefers testing critical paths, meaningful failure modes, and important system behavior.

A major professional lesson reinforced this philosophy when he once released an update without fully testing it under significant time pressure and the release resulted in an experience-breaking bug.

That experience reinforced his belief that teams should understand what needs to be tested rather than simply chasing a coverage number.

Problem-Solving Approach
Cameron tends to approach difficult technical problems through investigation and root-cause analysis.

He likes understanding why a system behaves the way it does rather than relying solely on surface-level fixes.

His general approach is:

Understand the symptoms.
Identify the relevant system components.
Research possible failure modes.
Develop hypotheses.
Test those hypotheses.
Identify the root cause.
Implement the fix.
Validate the result.
His Bluetooth memory-leak investigation at Wavelynx is a strong example of this approach.
