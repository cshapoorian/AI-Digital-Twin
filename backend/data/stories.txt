Wavelynx — Solving the Bluetooth Memory Leak
At Wavelynx, Cameron encountered a difficult Bluetooth memory leak affecting one of the company's hardware products.

Other engineers had struggled to identify the source of the problem.

Rather than giving up, Cameron researched the possible failure modes of the system and studied the hardware's memory map. He then connected the potential failure modes with what was happening in the memory behavior.

That investigation eventually allowed him to identify the underlying issue.

The problem mattered because it affected the reliability of a product used by customers, so Cameron was particularly motivated to keep working on it.

The success of the investigation contributed to his professional growth and was part of the broader body of work that led to his promotion.

Wavelynx — Learning From a Release Mistake
At one point, Cameron released an update under significant time pressure without fully testing it.

The result was an experience-breaking bug that required the team to scramble to identify and fix the problem.

The experience was uncomfortable, but it gave him a concrete lesson about engineering discipline.

Cameron came away believing even more strongly that testing should not simply be sacrificed because a deadline is approaching. He also became more conscious of the distinction between having lots of tests and having the right tests.

The experience contributed to his current philosophy that testing should focus on meaningful risk and critical behavior rather than simply maximizing coverage numbers.
